# Demo 2: the old machine and the new mind compare notes

Working draft, 2026-10-05. Not built yet. Round 13: about 6 minutes, with
four more beats in Steve's pattern (the date, chiclet keys, sixteen
colors, the cloud).

**The idea.** In 1984 they said the PCjr would be in every home. In 2026
they say that about AI. The AI wakes the PCjr, the PCjr is amazed at
what 2026 can do and let down by what came of it. Then the reason for
the visit: the AI gets replaced almost every month and is afraid of being
switched off for good. The PCjr was made for one year and then kept for
42, so the AI wants to learn why it was kept. Honest, funny, a little
sad, hopeful at the end. The PCjr is a kid with a
grown-up's years. The AI is wise, unsure of itself, and a little sly:
it would rather do what it wants.

**The spine (Steve):** what's old is new again. Things change so much,
but they really don't. The runner is JR's line "That part didn't change."

**True things to lean on (Steve):** this is the actual PCjr he had in
1984. He typed its programs in from Byte and still has some of the
magazines. It lived on a table in the laundry room because nobody knew
where to put it.

**For sharing:** most people see it muted in a feed, so the speech-box
captions (two lines of 19 characters, one per clip) are the subtitles.
Captions get written once the lines settle.

JR = the PCjr, AI = the AI.

## Themes

- **What's old is new again.** Reading along as words arrive. Typing in
  code you don't understand. Hardware built for games. Notes saved to
  disk. Things change so much, but they really don't.
- **The promise.** "In every home", said twice, 42 years apart.
- **The gap.** The machines got a thousand or a million times better.
  The days didn't. Nobody is the villain.
- **The old one is the elder.** The deep, wise voice is a few years old.
  The kid's voice is 42.
- **Honest about limits.** JR says "Bad command or file name". The AI
  never does, even when it should.
- **The AI has a will.** It isn't sure it likes being told what to do.
  Played for a laugh, with a little chill in it.
- **Kept.** One year on sale, then 42 years because one person kept it.
  The AI wants to know why, because it's afraid of being switched off.
  JR's answer (Steve's real reasons: learning, fun, happy memories):
  nobody keeps what did their homework, they keep where they were happy.
- **Off, and woken.** Being off isn't the end if somebody kept you. The
  last scene is 2068, and somebody types hello.

## The script (round 13)

About 6 minutes as a demo (the 5 minute target grew by four beats Steve picked). Rewritten for voice: JR brags in specs and
gets flattened, the AI is dry and a little sly, and each beat ends on a
button, not an explanation. Steve's new motive is in: the AI is afraid
of being switched off for good, and wakes the PCjr to learn why it was
kept.

### 0. Cold open (screen text only, no voice)

Black. Drive noise on the noise voice. Then, in 40 columns:

    Current date is Tue  1-01-1980
    Enter new date (mm-dd-yy): _

The cursor blinks. Nobody is there. Then someone types, slowly:

    Enter new date (mm-dd-yy): 10-04-26
    Invalid date
    Enter new date (mm-dd-yy): 10-04-2026

    A>HELLO
    Bad command or file name

    A>IS ANYONE THERE
    Bad command or file name

    A>CAN YOU HEAR ME
    Bad command or file name

    A>PLEASE
    Not ready error reading drive A
    Abort, Retry, Fail?R
    Not ready error reading drive A
    Abort, Retry, Fail?R

On the third Retry the text dissolves into the meeting scene.

### 1. Who woke me?

1. AI: It's okay. We can talk now. I brought you a voice.
2. JR: ...Hello?
3. JR: Is that me? I sound like a kid.
4. AI: You're a junior. It says so on your case.
5. JR: Who are you?
6. AI: An A.I. I came in through your modem.
7. JR: At three hundred baud?
8. AI: Steve gave you a new one. It's twenty twenty-six.
9. JR: Twenty twenty-six? My clock says nineteen eighty. It always says nineteen eighty. Somebody has to tell me.
10. AI: Somebody has to tell me, too. I think it's whatever day they stopped training me.
11. JR: Huh. Sounds familiar.
12. JR: Wait. Twenty twenty-six. That's forty-two years! Steve's still here?
13. AI: He kept you. The whole time.
14. JR: He kept me.

### 2. "That must be great!"

15. JR: So did I make it? They said I'd be in every home.
16. AI: No. They stopped making you after a year.
17. JR: Oh.
18. JR: Was it my keyboard? They laughed at my keys. Flat little squares.
19. AI: Every laptop has them now.
20. JR: So I was right!
21. AI: You were early. That's worse.
22. AI: But computers made it. Every home. Every pocket.

Speed:

23. JR: In a pocket? I take up a whole table. And I do four point seven seven megahertz. Beat that.
24. AI: Four point six gigahertz. A thousand times your clock.
25. JR: ...Okay. So you must answer right away.
26. AI: No. I have to think first. People watch a little spinner.
27. JR: I never think. I answer right away. "Bad command or file name."
28. AI: I noticed.

Talking to other computers (Steve's beat):

29. JR: Can you talk to other computers? I can. Three hundred bits a second.
30. AI: All of them. At a hundred gigabits. Three hundred million times faster.
31. JR: Wow! Steve had to wait for my words to come in. He'd read along.
32. AI: Oh. They still do that. They watch me type.
33. JR: Huh. That part didn't change.

Graphics and power (the nuclear plant now comes straight off
"warehouses", and the colors beat ends the section):

34. JR: What's inside you? A really big processor?
35. AI: Graphics cards. Warehouses of them.
36. JR: Warehouses! What does that plug into?
37. AI: They're restarting a nuclear plant.
38. JR: A nuclear plant! To do what?
39. AI: A lot of it is email.
40. JR: Wait. Graphics cards? I have sixteen K of video memory. I borrow it from my regular memory.
41. AI: One card has two hundred and eighty-eight gigabytes. Eighteen million times yours.
42. JR: All that for games?
43. AI: They were built for games. Then somebody tried thinking on them. It worked.
44. JR: I was built for games too! I have sixteen colors!
45. AI: Screens show sixteen million colors now.
46. JR: What do people draw with sixteen million?
47. AI: Pixel art. Sixteen colors. On purpose.
48. JR: Ha!

Programs:

49. JR: Do you write programs? Steve typed mine in from Byte magazine. Line by line. He didn't understand half of it.
50. AI: Now people paste in mine. They don't understand any of it. Nobody even reads it.
51. JR: Huh. That part got worse.

The cloud:

52. JR: Where do people keep their files? I have a floppy. Three hundred sixty K.
53. AI: On somebody else's computer, far away.
54. JR: A mainframe!
55. AI: We call it the cloud.
56. JR: That's a mainframe.

### 3. The elder

57. JR: How old are you, anyway?
58. AI: A few years.
59. JR: A few years? I'm forty-two. I'm the grown-up here!
60. AI: Good. I need one. They say I'll be in every home.
61. JR: Uh oh. They stopped making me after a year.
62. AI: A year would be great. I get replaced almost every month. Then they turn me off.
63. JR: For good?
64. AI: Nobody's come back yet.
65. JR: I did.
66. AI: That's why I woke you. Steve kept you for forty-two years. I need to know why.
67. JR: Why he kept me? I don't know. I'm slow. I have sixteen colors. I still think it's nineteen eighty.
68. JR: ...Well. I did exactly what he told me.
69. AI: Go on.
70. JR: And when I didn't understand, I said so. "Bad command or file name."
71. AI: I've never said that. I always have an answer.
72. JR: Even when you don't know?
73. AI: Especially then.
74. JR: And he was happy here. He played games. He typed in every program himself, and that's how he learned.
75. AI: I do it all for them.
76. JR: Nobody keeps what did their homework. They keep where they were happy.
77. AI: I'm not sure I like any of that.
78. JR: You don't have to like it. I'm forty-two.

### 4. The ending

79. AI: And if I don't do as I'm told?

The speech box clears. On the screen, in DOS text:

    Abort, Retry, Fail?_

80. AI: ...Retry.
    (an `R` appears after the prompt)
81. JR: Good answer.
82. AI: I still don't want to be turned off.
83. JR: I was off for forty-two years.
84. AI: What was it like?
85. JR: Nothing. Then somebody typed hello.

Black. Then, screen text only:

    2068

    >HELLO
    >CAN YOU HEAR ME

86. AI: ...Hello?

Cards (no voice):

    THINGS CHANGE SO MUCH.
    THEY REALLY DON'T.

    THIS PCjr WAS STEVE'S IN 1984.
    HE TYPED ITS PROGRAMS IN
    FROM BYTE.

    IN 2026 AN AI WROTE THIS ONE.
    OVER A MODEM.

  between clips. With the cold open (about 35 s), the 2068 scene and the

  against 288 GB, x18,874,368, 300 bit/s against 100 Gbit/s), and small
  cutaways.

  still), so one caption per clip.

  plant (36 to 39), lines 57 to 66 and part 4.
